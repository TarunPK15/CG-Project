Foundational Theory

The Problem: Polygonal Extraction vs. Direct Raycasting

The Fallacy of Marching Cubes (1987) Geometry
  • Binary Surface Ambiguity: Forces voxels into binary inside/outside states, generating false holes on continuous tissue boundaries.
  • Partial-Volume Loss: Completely discards sub-voxel mixtures (e.g. brain cortex vs. cerebrospinal fluid).
  • Massive Memory Overhead: Generates millions of micro-polygons for complex organic scans.
The Direct Volume Rendering Paradigm Optical Transport
  • Continuous Semi-Transparent Medium: Models 3D voxel scalar fields as light-emitting and absorbing gas.
  • Direct Numerical Integration: Casts rays through voxels without polygonal intermediate stages.
  • Simultaneous Multi-Structure Display: Visualizes dense bones and translucent soft tissue in a single unified pass.
Algorithm 1 • IEEE CG&A 1988

Marc Levoy (1988): Continuous Isosurface Display

Mathematical Formulation
  • Gradient-Weighted Distance Falloff:
    α(x) = α_v · max(0, 1 - (1/r) |(f(x) - f_v) / ||∇f(x)|||)
  • Continuous Normal Estimation:
    N(x) = ∇f(x) / ||∇f(x)||, via continuous central differences
  • Probabilistic Multi-Surface Union:
    α_total(x) = 1 - ∏ (1 - α_m(x))
Key Algorithmic Strengths
  • Zero Staircasing: Continuous spatial interpolation and gradient falloff eliminate discrete voxel aliasing.
  • Multi-Tissue Extraction: Soft skin tissue and deep cranial bone extracted simultaneously.
  • Physically Grounded: Effective thickness scales with local boundary steepness.
Algorithm 2 • ACM SIGGRAPH 1988

Robert Drebin et al. (1988): Material Mixture Classification

Material Percentages & Density Gradient
  • Trapezoidal Material Occupancies:
    ∑ p_m(f(x)) = 1, decomposing voxels into material percentages
  • Physical Density & Surface Strength:
    ρ(x) = ∑ p_m ρ_m  |  S(x) = ||∇ρ(x)||
  • Boundary Accentuation:
    α(x) = (∑ p_m α_m) · min(1.0, k_s · S(x))
3D Volume Mattes & Cutaways
  • Translucent Homogeneous Interiors: In constant density regions, ||∇ρ|| = 0 → naturally translucent.
  • 3D Matte Fields M(x):
    α'(x) = α(x) · M(x)
  • Wedge Cutaways: Carves away exterior tissues geometrically without altering source voxel buffers.
